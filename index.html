About Our Organization

Dedicated to Foothill Public Safety

The Sierra Madre Fire Safe Council (SMFSC) is a community-based, nonprofit organization dedicated to reducing wildfire risk and enhancing public safety in the foothill areas of Sierra Madre, California.

Through education, outreach, and partnerships with local government agencies and neighborhood groups, the SMFSC works to help residents adopt fire-resilient practices such as defensible space, home hardening, and fuel reduction. By fostering community awareness and collective action, it aims to protect lives, property, and the natural landscape from wildfire threats.

All contributions directly support local resilience initiatives.

Wildfire Action Framework

Ready, Set, Go!

Living in Sierra Madre’s Wildland Urban Interface means preparing well ahead of fire weather, staying alert, and acting without hesitation.

1

Get Ready

Prepare your property long before a fire starts by creating a buffer.

  • Create Defensible Space (Zones 1 & 2)
  • Clean gutters and roof of dry pine needles and debris
  • Install ember-resistant 1/8" metal mesh vents
  • Choose fire-smart, high-moisture landscaping
Book property inspection →
2

Get Set

Assemble your emergency supplies and build a family action plan.

  • Build an Emergency "Go Bag" for each family member
  • Establish multiple vehicular and pedestrian escape routes
  • Designate a safe out-of-area family meeting spot
  • Prepare an animal evacuation plan and pet crates
3

Go!

Act early when wildfire strikes. Do not wait for a mandatory order.

  • Leave early to prevent road gridlock for emergency apparatus
  • Dress in protective cotton/wool layers, boots, and N95 mask
  • Shut off gas at the primary meter if safe to do so
  • Leave exterior gates unlocked for first responders

Block Captains Program

Block Captains are the local link between the Sierra Madre Fire Safe Council (SMFSC) and their immediate neighborhood. It’s a simple volunteer role that helps keep our community informed, prepared, and connected in the face of wildfire risk. Responsibilities include sharing fire preparedness tips, helping neighbors stay updated on safety projects, and assisting with occasional outreach (like distributing flyers or collecting input).

Emergency Planning Guide

Have an Emergency Plan & Build a Kit

Basic Protective Measures for All Hazards

  • Physical safety is a concern for all hazards and may involve sheltering or immediate evacuating.
  • Develop a family communications and reuniting plan with designated emergency contacts.
  • Make an emergency supply kit to be prepared for all disaster categories.
  • Sign up to receive emergency alerts via cell phone and email.
  • Learn local evacuation routes, safety refuge areas, and warning alert protocols.
  • When recovering from a disaster, prioritize psychological well-being alongside structural recovery.

7-Day Supply Kit Essentials

Pre-assemble these items in portable, durable packs before red flag conditions arise:

💧 Water: 1 gal/person/day for at least 7 days
🥫 Food: 7-day non-perishable supply & manual opener
📻 Battery/hand-crank & NOAA weather radio
🔦 Flashlight with abundant extra batteries
🩹 Comprehensive first aid kit & whistle
😷 Dust masks (N95) & plastic sheeting/duct tape
🧻 Moist towelettes & heavy-duty garbage bags
🔧 Wrench or pliers to safely turn off gas/utilities
🗺️ Physical local topographic/evacuation maps
🔋 Cell phone cords, power banks, or solar charger

Additional Critical Items to Add

  • Prescription medications (at least 14 days) and spare eyeglasses.
  • Infant formula, bottles, and diapers.
  • Pet food, leash, extra water, and travel crate.
  • Copies of vital identification, deeds, and insurance policies in a waterproof container.
  • Cash in small denominations ($1, $5, $10, $20 bills).
  • Sleeping bag or warm wool blanket for each individual.
  • Sturdy change of clothing: long-sleeve cotton/wool, durable pants, work boots.
  • Household liquid chlorine bleach (plain, unscented) + dropper for disinfectant (9:1) or emergency water sanitization (16 drops per gallon).
  • Fire extinguisher and matches in waterproof case.
